## 1. Overview

A **fluid** is a substance in the liquid or gas phase that deforms continuously under any applied shear stress, no matter how small. In **GET 205**, every piping, dam-gate, and pump calculation rests on two foundations from **Çengel's *Fundamentals of Thermal-Fluid Sciences***: **fluid properties** (Chapter 10) and **fluid statics & manometry** (Chapters 2 & 11).

## 2. Key Concepts

### Fundamental Fluid Properties (Çengel Ch. 10)

- **Density ($\rho$) & Specific Gravity ($SG$):** Mass per unit volume ($\text{kg/m}^3$), with standard water density $\rho_{\text{H}_2\text{O}} = 1000\,\text{kg/m}^3$:
  $$SG = \frac{\rho}{\rho_{\text{H}_2\text{O}}}, \qquad \gamma = \rho g \;\;(\text{N/m}^3)$$
- **Newton's Law of Viscosity:** For laminar shear between parallel layers with velocity gradient $\frac{du}{dy}$, the shear stress $\tau$ ($\text{Pa}$ or $\text{N/m}^2$) is:
  $$\tau = \mu \frac{du}{dy}, \qquad \nu = \frac{\mu}{\rho} \;\;(\text{m}^2\text{/s})$$
  where $\mu$ is **dynamic viscosity** ($\text{kg/m}\cdot\text{s}$ or $\text{Pa}\cdot\text{s}$) and $\nu$ is **kinematic viscosity**.

### Hydrostatic Pressure & Differential Manometers (Çengel §2–7 to §2–9)

In a static incompressible fluid, pressure increases linearly with depth ($\Delta P = \rho g h$) and remains identical at all points on the same horizontal plane connected by the same continuous fluid (**Pascal's Law**). Across a horizontal flow section tapped by a differential U-tube manometer (fluid density $\rho_1$, heavy gage fluid $\rho_2$, column differential $h$):

$$P_1 - P_2 = (\rho_2 - \rho_1)\,g h$$

### Hydrostatic Forces on Submerged Plane Plates (Çengel §11–2)

For a submerged rectangular plate of width $a$ and height $b$ tilted at angle $\theta$ from the horizontal with its top edge at slant distance $s$ from the free surface (surface pressure $P_0$), the resultant hydrostatic force equals the centroid pressure $P_C$ times area $A = ab$:

$$F_R = P_C A = \left[P_0 + \rho g\!\left(s + \frac{b}{2}\right)\sin\theta\right]ab$$

When atmospheric pressure $P_0$ acts on both sides and cancels ($P_0 = 0$), the **center of pressure** $y_P$ along the plate lies below the centroid at:

$$y_P = s + \frac{b}{2} + \frac{b^2}{12(s + b/2)}$$

## 4. Common Exam Pitfalls

<Callout variant="warning" title="Three high-frequency GET 205 mistakes">
- **Jumping across two different fluids in a manometer:** You may only equate pressures horizontally at $A$ and $B$ if a **single continuous static fluid** connects $A$ and $B$ without an intervening meniscus.
- **Forgetting to convert $\text{Pa}$ to $\text{kPa}$ before adding $P_{\text{atm}}$:** $\rho g h$ in SI base units always yields **Pascals ($\text{N/m}^2$)**—divide by $1000$ before adding to $96\,\text{kPa}$ or $101.3\,\text{kPa}$!
- **Confusing centroid depth $h_C$ with pressure center $y_P$:** Force magnitude uses $h_C$, whereas moment/hinge equilibrium uses the deeper line of action $y_P$.
</Callout>
