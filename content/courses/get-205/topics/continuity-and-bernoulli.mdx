## 1. Overview

Once a fluid starts moving through pipes, nozzles, or over an aircraft wing, **conservation of mass** (continuity) and **conservation of mechanical energy** (Bernoulli's equation) become your two primary workhorses. Together they explain why squeezing a garden hose speeds up the jet, how a Venturi meter measures campus water flow, and how a Pitot tube measures airspeed.

---

## 2. Key Concepts

### Conservation of Mass (Continuity Equation)

For steady, incompressible flow ($\rho = \text{constant}$) along a streamtube or pipe with no leaks, the volumetric flow rate $Q$ ($\text{m}^3\text{/s}$) stays constant at every cross-section:

$$Q = A_1 V_1 = A_2 V_2$$

For circular pipes of diameters $D_1$ and $D_2$:

$$\frac{V_2}{V_1} = \frac{A_1}{A_2} = \left(\frac{D_1}{D_2}\right)^2$$

Halving the pipe diameter increases the average fluid velocity by a factor of **$4\times$**!

### Bernoulli's Equation

Along a streamline for **steady, incompressible, inviscid (frictionless)** flow with no shaft work or heat transfer:

$$P_1 + \frac{1}{2}\rho V_1^2 + \rho g z_1 = P_2 + \frac{1}{2}\rho V_2^2 + \rho g z_2$$

Dividing every term by specific weight $\rho g$ gives the **head form** (every term in **metres**):

$$\frac{P_1}{\rho g} + \frac{V_1^2}{2g} + z_1 = \frac{P_2}{\rho g} + \frac{V_2^2}{2g} + z_2$$

- $\frac{P}{\rho g}$ = **pressure head**
- $\frac{V^2}{2g}$ = **velocity head**
- $z$ = **elevation (potential) head**

### Flow Measurement Devices

1. **Pitot-Static Tube (Stagnation Velocity):** Fluid stops ($V_2 = 0$) at the stagnation nose while static pressure $P_1$ is tapped along the side wall:
   $$V_1 = \sqrt{\frac{2(P_{\text{stag}} - P_{\text{static}})}{\rho}} = \sqrt{2 g \Delta h}$$

2. **Horizontal Venturi Meter:** Combining continuity $V_1 = (A_2/A_1)V_2$ with Bernoulli between inlet (1) and throat (2) yields the ideal discharge:
   $$Q_{\text{ideal}} = A_2 \sqrt{\frac{2(P_1 - P_2)}{\rho \left[1 - (A_2/A_1)^2\right]}}$$
   Actual discharge multiplies $Q_{\text{ideal}}$ by the discharge coefficient $C_d$ (typically $0.95\text{–}0.99$).

<Callout variant="tip" title="Always state Bernoulli's 4 assumptions in theory questions">
OAU examiners award 2–4 marks just for listing them cleanly: **(1) Steady flow, (2) Incompressible fluid ($\rho = \text{const}$), (3) Inviscid / frictionless flow, (4) Applied along a single streamline.**
</Callout>

## 4. Common Exam Pitfalls

<Callout variant="warning" title="Don't lose easy marks here">
- **Mixing $\text{kPa}$ and $\text{Pa}$:** The dynamic term $\frac{1}{2}\rho V^2$ produces **Pascals**, never kilopascals. Always convert $P_1$ to $\text{Pa}$ first, then divide by $1000$ at the end.
- **Forgetting to square the diameter ratio:** $V_2/V_1 = (D_1/D_2)^2$, NOT $D_1/D_2$.
</Callout>
