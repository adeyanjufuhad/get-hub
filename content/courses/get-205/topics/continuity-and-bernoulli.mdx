## 1. Overview

In moving fluids, velocity and pressure trade places whenever flow area or elevation changes. **Çengel Chapter 12** pairs **conservation of mass** with the **Bernoulli equation**—giving us direct analytical formulas for high-speed hose jets, tank drainage (Torricelli's equation), siphons, Pitot-static velocity probes, and Venturi flow meters.

---

## 2. Key Concepts

### Continuity for Steady Incompressible Flow

When density $\rho$ is constant, volumetric flow rate $\dot{V} = Q$ ($\text{m}^3\text{/s}$) is conserved between inlet (1) and outlet (2):

$$Q = A_1 V_1 = A_2 V_2 \implies V_2 = V_1 \left(\frac{D_1}{D_2}\right)^2$$

### Bernoulli's Equation & Mechanical Energy Forms (Çengel §12–2)

Along a streamline in **steady, incompressible, frictionless (inviscid)** flow with zero shaft work:

$$\frac{P_1}{\rho} + \frac{V_1^2}{2} + g z_1 = \frac{P_2}{\rho} + \frac{V_2^2}{2} + g z_2 \qquad (\text{J/kg})$$

Dividing through by $g$ converts every term into **head** (metres of fluid):

$$\frac{P_1}{\rho g} + \frac{V_1^2}{2g} + z_1 = \frac{P_2}{\rho g} + \frac{V_2^2}{2g} + z_2 \qquad (\text{m})$$

### Static, Stagnation & Pitot-Tube Measurement (Çengel §12–2 & §12–3)

Bringing a moving stream isentropically to rest ($V_2 = 0$) converts dynamic pressure $\frac{1}{2}\rho V_1^2$ into **stagnation pressure** $P_{\text{stag}} = P + \frac{1}{2}\rho V^2$:

$$V = \sqrt{\frac{2(P_{\text{stag}} - P)}{\rho}} = \sqrt{2 g h_3}$$

where $h_3$ is the excess liquid column rise in the Pitot tube above the wall piezometer tube!

## 4. Common Exam Pitfalls

<Callout variant="warning" title="Key Bernoulli checks in GET 205">
- **Adding $h_1 + h_2 + h_3$ in a Pitot-piezometer problem:** Only the **differential height $h_3$** between the stagnation column and the static piezometer column represents the velocity head $\frac{V_1^2}{2g}$!
- **Free jet pressure is atmospheric ($P_{\text{gage}} = 0$):** The instant water exits a nozzle or tank tap into open air, its pressure equals $P_{\text{atm}}$—never carry the internal tank gauge pressure outside the orifice.
</Callout>
