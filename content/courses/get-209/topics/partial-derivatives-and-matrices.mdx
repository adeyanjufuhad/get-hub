## 1. Overview

Multivariable calculus and matrix algebra power nearly every upper-level engineering model—from heat-conduction gradients in CHE/MEE to nodal circuit matrices in EEE/CPE. This guide combines **Stewart Chapter 14 (§14.3–14.6)** for partial derivatives, total differentials, and gradients with **Bird Chapter 60** for matrices and determinants.

## 2. Key Concepts

### Partial Derivatives & Geometric Surface Traces (Stewart §14.3–14.4)

For $z = f(x, y)$:
- Hold $y$ fixed and differentiate with respect to $x$ to get $f_x(a, b)$ — the tangent slope along trace $C_1$ (plane $y = b$).
- Hold $x$ fixed and differentiate with respect to $y$ to get $f_y(a, b)$ — the tangent slope along trace $C_2$ (plane $x = a$).
- **Total Differential:** $dz = f_x(x, y)\,dx + f_y(x, y)\,dy$.

### Gradient Vector & Directional Derivative (Stewart §14.6)

The **gradient vector** $\nabla f(x, y) = \langle f_x, f_y \rangle = \frac{\partial f}{\partial x}\mathbf{i} + \frac{\partial f}{\partial y}\mathbf{j}$ points perpendicular to level curves in the direction of **maximum rate of increase** (with maximum slope $|\nabla f|$). Along any unit vector $\mathbf{u} = \frac{\mathbf{v}}{|\mathbf{v}|}$:

$$D_{\mathbf{u}} f(x, y) = \nabla f(x, y) \cdot \mathbf{u}$$

### Matrices & Determinants (Bird Ch. 60)

- Matrix multiplication is **non-commutative** ($AB \neq BA$ in general).
- For $A = \begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{pmatrix}$, $\det(A) = ad - bc$ and $A^{-1} = \frac{1}{ad - bc}\begin{pmatrix} d & -b \\ -c & a \end{pmatrix}$ provided $\det(A) \neq 0$.

## 3. Textbook Worked Examples

<StepRevealExample
  title="Stewart §14.3 Example 1 — Evaluating fx(2, 1) and fy(2, 1)"
  problem="If $f(x, y) = x^3 + x^2 y^3 - 2y^2$, find $f_x(2, 1)$ and $f_y(2, 1)$."
  steps={[
    "Hold $y$ constant and differentiate with respect to $x$: $f_x(x, y) = 3x^2 + 2x y^3$.",
    "Substitute $(2, 1)$: $f_x(2, 1) = 3(2)^2 + 2(2)(1)^3 = 12 + 4 = 16$.",
    "Hold $x$ constant and differentiate with respect to $y$: $f_y(x, y) = 3x^2 y^2 - 4y$.",
    "Substitute $(2, 1)$: $f_y(2, 1) = 3(2)^2(1)^2 - 4(1) = 12 - 4 = 8$."
  ]}
/>

<StepRevealExample
  title="Stewart §14.6 Example 4 — Directional Derivative Along v = 2i + 5j (Fig. 6)"
  problem="Find the directional derivative of $f(x, y) = x^2 y^3 - 4y$ at the point $(2, -1)$ in the direction of the vector $\mathbf{v} = 2\mathbf{i} + 5\mathbf{j}$."
  steps={[
    "Compute the gradient vector $\\nabla f(x, y) = 2x y^3\\,\\mathbf{i} + (3x^2 y^2 - 4)\\,\\mathbf{j}$.",
    "Evaluate at $(2, -1)$: $\\nabla f(2, -1) = 2(2)(-1)^3\\,\\mathbf{i} + \\bigl(3(4)(1) - 4\\bigr)\\,\\mathbf{j} = -4\\mathbf{i} + 8\\mathbf{j}$.",
    "Normalize $\\mathbf{v}$ into a unit vector: $|\\mathbf{v}| = \\sqrt{2^2 + 5^2} = \\sqrt{29} \\implies \\mathbf{u} = \\frac{2}{\\sqrt{29}}\\mathbf{i} + \\frac{5}{\\sqrt{29}}\\mathbf{j}$.",
    "Take the dot product: $D_{\\mathbf{u}} f(2, -1) = (-4)\\!\\left(\\frac{2}{\\sqrt{29}}\\right) + 8\\!\\left(\\frac{5}{\\sqrt{29}}\\right) = \\frac{-8 + 40}{\\sqrt{29}} = \\frac{32}{\\sqrt{29}} \\approx 5.94$."
  ]}
/>

## 4. Common Exam Pitfalls

<Callout variant="warning" title="Always normalize v before taking the dot product!">
In Stewart §14.6 Example 4, dotting $\nabla f(2, -1) = \langle -4, 8 \rangle$ directly with $\mathbf{v} = \langle 2, 5 \rangle$ gives $32$, which is off by a factor of $\sqrt{29}$! Always divide by $|\mathbf{v}|$ first.
</Callout>
