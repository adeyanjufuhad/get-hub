## 1. Overview

Whether you are designing a bridge girder in Civil, a motor shaft in Mechanical, or a heavy server rack in Computer/Electrical Engineering, your first question is always the same: **will this thing stay put without breaking or tipping over?**

Applied Mechanics (GET 207) begins with **Statics**—balancing forces and turning effects (moments) so a structure remains in equilibrium. Nearly every later topic (trusses, friction, shear-force diagrams, and even dynamics via Newton's laws) depends on mastering Free-Body Diagrams here.

## 2. Key Concepts

### Resolving Coplanar Forces

Any 2D force of magnitude $F$ acting at angle $\theta$ measured from the positive $x$-axis splits cleanly into rectangular components:

$$F_x = F \cos\theta, \qquad F_y = F \sin\theta$$

When several forces act at a point, sum their components independently to obtain the resultant $\vec{R}$:

$$R_x = \sum F_x, \qquad R_y = \sum F_y, \qquad R = \sqrt{R_x^2 + R_y^2}, \qquad \tan\alpha = \frac{R_y}{R_x}$$

### Moments and Couples

A **moment** measures a force's tendency to rotate a body about a pivot point $O$:

$$M_O = F \cdot d_\perp$$

where $d_\perp$ is the **perpendicular distance** from $O$ to the force's line of action. By convention, **anticlockwise (CCW)** moments are positive ($+$) and **clockwise (CW)** moments are negative ($-$).

A **force couple** is a special pair of equal, parallel, opposite forces separated by perpendicular distance $d$. Its net force is zero, yet it produces a pure turning moment $M = F \cdot d$ that has the **exact same value about every point in the plane**.

### 2D Static Equilibrium & Free-Body Diagrams

To keep a rigid body completely still in a 2D plane—no sliding horizontally, no sliding vertically, and no rotating—three scalar equations must hold simultaneously:

$$\sum F_x = 0, \qquad \sum F_y = 0, \qquad \sum M_O = 0$$

## 4. Common Mistakes

<Callout variant="warning" title="Watch out for these exam traps">
- **Mixing up clockwise and anticlockwise signs:** Write a tiny `(+ CCW)` symbol next to every $\sum M = 0$ line before plugging in numbers.
- **Using slanted distance instead of perpendicular distance:** A moment is $F \cdot d_\perp$, not $F$ times any random beam length. If a force is angled, resolve it into components first!
- **Forgetting to check $\sum M_B = 0$:** Spending 15 seconds checking moments about the other support catches 95% of arithmetic slips in tests.
</Callout>
