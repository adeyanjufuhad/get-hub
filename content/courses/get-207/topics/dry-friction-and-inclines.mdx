## 1. Overview

In real engineering machines—brakes, clutches, bolted joints, jackscrews, and wedges—surfaces are never perfectly smooth. **Dry (Coulomb) friction** is a self-adjusting tangential force that opposes *impending* or *actual* relative sliding between two dry surfaces in contact.

The #1 mistake students make in GET 207 friction problems is immediately writing $F = \mu_s N$ before checking whether the body is actually on the verge of slipping!

---

## 2. Key Concepts

### Coulomb's Laws of Dry Friction

1. **No slipping (static equilibrium):** Friction $F$ takes whatever magnitude equilibrium demands, up to a strict ceiling:
   $$0 \le F \le F_{\max} = \mu_s N$$
2. **Impending motion (verge of slipping):** At the exact threshold of motion:
   $$F = \mu_s N$$
3. **Sliding in progress (kinetic friction):** Once sliding begins, friction drops slightly to:
   $$F_k = \mu_k N \qquad (\mu_k < \mu_s)$$

### Angle of Static Friction & Angle of Repose

Combine the normal reaction $N$ and limiting friction $F_{\max} = \mu_s N$ into a single resultant contact force $R$ inclined at angle $\phi_s$ from the normal:

$$\tan\phi_s = \frac{F_{\max}}{N} = \mu_s$$

If a block rests freely on an incline tilted gradually upward, it begins to slide downward when the incline angle $\theta$ reaches the **angle of repose** $\theta_s$:

$$\tan\theta_s = \mu_s \implies \theta_s = \phi_s$$

### Inclined Plane Force Resolution

Align your $x$-axis **parallel** to the incline and $y$-axis **perpendicular** to it:
- Weight component down the plane: $W\sin\theta$
- Weight component into the plane: $W\cos\theta$
- **To push a block UP the plane** with a force $P$ parallel to the slope, friction acts **down** the slope:
  $$P_{\text{up}} = W\sin\theta + \mu_s W\cos\theta$$
- **Minimum force to hold a block from sliding DOWN**, friction helps you by acting **up** the slope:
  $$P_{\text{hold}} = W\sin\theta - \mu_s W\cos\theta$$

<Callout variant="tip" title="Friction direction rule">
Ask: *"If all friction vanished right now, which way would this surface slide?"* Draw the friction arrow in the **exact opposite** direction.
</Callout>

## 4. Common Exam Pitfalls

<Callout variant="warning" title="Avoid the #1 friction trap">
Never assume $N = W$ when an external force $P$ is applied **at an angle** to the surface or horizontally against a ramp! Always resolve perpendicular forces ($\sum F_y = 0$) first to get the true normal force $N$.
</Callout>
