## 1. Overview

Objects feel lighter in water because fluid pressure increases with depth, pushing upward harder on the bottom of a submerged body than downward on its top. In **Çengel Chapter 11 (§11–4)**, this net upward **buoyant force** governs underwater crane loads, hydrometer calibration, and whether a floating pontoon or ship rights itself or capsizes when tilted by waves.

---

## 2. Key Concepts

### Archimedes' Principle (Çengel §11–4)

The buoyant force $F_B$ acting on a body immersed in a fluid of density $\rho_f$ equals the weight of the fluid displaced by the body, acting upward through the **center of buoyancy $B$** (the centroid of the displaced volume $V_{\text{sub}}$):

$$F_B = \rho_f \, g \, V_{\text{sub}}$$

For a **floating body** of average density $\rho_{\text{body}}$ and total volume $V$, static equilibrium ($F_B = W$) dictates that the submerged volume fraction equals the density ratio:

$$\frac{V_{\text{sub}}}{V} = \frac{\rho_{\text{body}}}{\rho_f}$$

### Rotational Stability & Metacentric Height $GM$

- **Completely immersed body (submarine, balloon):** Stable only when bottom-heavy so the center of gravity $G$ lies **directly below** the center of buoyancy $B$.
- **Floating body (ship hull, pontoon):** Even when $G$ sits above $B$, rolling through a small angle shifts the displaced wedge sideways so the center of buoyancy moves to $B'$. Let **metacenter $M$** be the intersection of the new buoyant line of action with the hull's symmetry axis:

$$BM = \frac{I_{\min}}{V_{\text{sub}}}, \qquad GM = BM - BG$$

- **$GM > 0$ ($M$ above $G$):** **Stable** — $W$ and $F_B$ form a **restoring moment** that rights the vessel.
- **$GM < 0$ ($M$ below $G$):** **Unstable** — $W$ and $F_B$ form an **overturning moment** that capsizes the vessel.

## 4. Common Exam Pitfalls

<Callout variant="warning" title="Don't trip on these buoyancy checks">
- **Always use $\rho_{\text{fluid}}$ in $F_B = \rho_f g V_{\text{sub}}$:** The concrete density ($2300\,\text{kg/m}^3$) determines $W$, while seawater density ($1025\,\text{kg/m}^3$) determines $F_B$.
- **Rolling axis of a rectangular pontoon:** Always cube the **smaller** waterplane width $b$ in $I_{\min} = \frac{L b^3}{12}$, because a barge rolls sideways much more easily than it pitches end-over-end.
</Callout>
