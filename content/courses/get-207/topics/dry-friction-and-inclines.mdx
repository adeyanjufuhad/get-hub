## 1. Overview

In **Hibbeler Chapter 8**, dry (Coulomb) friction problems fall into two distinct classes: problems where impending motion is *known* to occur ($F = \mu_s N$ applies immediately), and problems where you **do not know** whether the crate stays at rest, slips, or tips over first!

---

## 2. Key Concepts

### Characteristics of Dry Friction (Hibbeler §8.1–8.2)

1. **Static Equilibrium (No Slipping):** Friction $F$ is found strictly from equilibrium equations ($\sum F_x = 0$) and must satisfy:
   $$F \le F_{\max} = \mu_s N$$
2. **Impending Motion (Verge of Slipping):** At the threshold of motion, $F$ acts opposite to impending slip with magnitude:
   $$F_s = \mu_s N$$
3. **Sliding Motion:** Once the body moves, friction drops to kinetic friction:
   $$F_k = \mu_k N$$
4. **Tipping Check:** On a flat base of width $2a$, the normal reaction $N_C$ shifts laterally by distance $x$ from the centerline to balance moments ($\sum M_O = 0$). If $|x| \le a$, the block does **not** tip; if $|x| > a$, tipping occurs about the corner!

### Leaning Rods, Ladders & Multi-Surface Contact

When a ladder or rod $AB$ leans against a rough wall ($A$) and floor ($B$), draw normal and friction forces at each rough contact opposing the direction that end would slide, then take moments about $B$ ($\sum M_B = 0$) to eliminate two unknowns in one step!

## 4. Common Exam Pitfalls

<Callout variant="warning" title="Don't fall for the P = 200 N vs. P = 400 N trap (Prob. F8–1)">
In Hibbeler **Prob. F8–1**, a downward-sloping force $P$ increases the normal force $N = mg + \frac{3}{5}P$. Always compute $F_{\max} = \mu_s N$ first: if $\frac{4}{5}P \le F_{\max}$, friction equals $\frac{4}{5}P$; if $\frac{4}{5}P > F_{\max}$, the crate slides and friction switches to **kinetic** $F_k = \mu_k N$!
</Callout>
