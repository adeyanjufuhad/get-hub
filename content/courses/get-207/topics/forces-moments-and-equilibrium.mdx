## 1. Overview

In **R. C. Hibbeler's *Engineering Mechanics: Statics & Dynamics* (14th Edition)**, every structural analysis begins with isolating the body in a **Free-Body Diagram (FBD)** and replacing pins, rollers, fixed bolts, and two-force struts with their exact reaction components before writing a single equation.

---

## 2. Key Concepts

### Varignon's Principle of Moments & Couple Moments (Hibbeler Ch. 4)

Rather than finding the slant perpendicular arm $d$ to an inclined force, **Varignon's Theorem (§4.4)** lets us resolve $\mathbf{F}$ into rectangular components $F_x, F_y$ and sum their moments algebraically ($+$ anticlockwise):

$$\left(M_R\right)_O = \sum F_y x - \sum F_x y$$

A **couple** consists of two parallel forces of equal magnitude $F$ and opposite sense separated by perpendicular arm $d$. Its net force is zero, and its **couple moment** $M = F d$ is a **free vector**—it exerts the exact same turning moment about every point on the structure!

### 2D Rigid-Body Equilibrium & Two-Force Members (Hibbeler Ch. 5)

A coplanar rigid body stays at rest when all external loads and support reactions satisfy:

$$\sum F_x = 0, \qquad \sum F_y = 0, \qquad \sum M_O = 0$$

- **Fixed support (bolt/weld):** Exerts **three** unknowns ($A_x$, $A_y$, and couple moment $M_A$).
- **Pin support:** Exerts **two** force unknowns ($A_x, A_y$).
- **Roller / smooth rocker / two-force link:** Exerts **one** force unknown along a known line of action!

## 4. Common Exam Pitfalls

<Callout variant="warning" title="Hibbeler's three equilibrium golden rules">
- **Spot two-force members first:** In Prob. F5–2, treating $C$ as two unknowns ($C_x, C_y$) makes the beam look statically indeterminate—recognizing strut $CD$ acts at $45^\circ$ reduces $C$ to a single unknown $F_{CD}$!
- **Include fixed-support couple moments $M_A$ in $\sum M$, NEVER in $\sum F$:** Couple moments have units of $\text{N}\cdot\text{m}$, not Newtons.
- **Check with a fourth moment equation:** Hibbeler always verifies answers by summing moments about a second point (like $\sum M_C = 0$).
</Callout>
