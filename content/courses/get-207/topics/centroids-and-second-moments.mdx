## 1. Overview

In structural mechanics, the **centroid** $(\bar{x}, \bar{y})$ locates the geometric center where distributed forces concentrate, while the **area moment of inertia** ($I_x, I_y$) governs resistance to beam bending and column buckling. **Hibbeler Chapters 9 and 10** reduce every flanged steel beam or perforated plate to simple rectangles and circles combined via the **Parallel-Axis Theorem**.

---

## 2. Key Concepts

### Composite Area Centroids (Hibbeler §9.2)

Subdivide a cross-section into standard parts (using negative area $A_i < 0$ for holes/cutouts):

$$\bar{x} = \frac{\sum \tilde{x}_i A_i}{\sum A_i}, \qquad \bar{y} = \frac{\sum \tilde{y}_i A_i}{\sum A_i}$$

### Parallel-Axis Theorem & Composite Inertia (Hibbeler §10.2 & §10.4)

If $\bar{I}_{x'}$ is the moment of inertia of sub-area $A$ about its **own centroidal axis** $x'$, its moment of inertia about any parallel axis $x$ at perpendicular distance $d_y$ is:

$$I_x = \bar{I}_{x'} + A d_y^2, \qquad k_x = \sqrt{\frac{I_x}{A}}$$

- **Rectangle ($b \times h$):** $\bar{I}_{x'} = \dfrac{1}{12} b h^3$
- **Circle (radius $r$):** $\bar{I}_{x'} = \dfrac{1}{4}\pi r^4$
- **Right Triangle ($b \times h$):** $\bar{I}_{x'} = \dfrac{1}{36} b h^3$

## 4. Common Exam Pitfalls

<Callout variant="warning" title="Subtract BOTH terms for cutouts!">
When subtracting a hole in Parallel-Axis problems (like Example 10.4), you must subtract the **entire** shifted quantity $\bigl(\bar{I}_{\text{hole}} + A_{\text{hole}} d^2\bigr)$—students frequently subtract $\bar{I}_{\text{hole}}$ and accidentally *add* $A_{\text{hole}} d^2$!
</Callout>
