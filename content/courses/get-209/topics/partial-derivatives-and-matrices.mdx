## 1. Overview

Real physical quantities rarely depend on just one variable—temperature varies with $(x, y, z)$, and truss or circuit nodal equations involve 3+ coupled unknowns simultaneously. This guide pulls together **partial derivatives, gradients**, and **matrix determinants**—three high-yield sections of GET 209 exams.

---

## 2. Key Concepts

### Partial Derivatives & Total Differential

For $z = f(x, y)$:
- $\dfrac{\partial f}{\partial x}$ differentiates with respect to $x$ while holding **$y$ constant**.
- $\dfrac{\partial f}{\partial y}$ differentiates with respect to $y$ while holding **$x$ constant**.

The **total differential** approximates small simultaneous changes $\Delta x, \Delta y$:

$$dz = \frac{\partial f}{\partial x}\,dx + \frac{\partial f}{\partial y}\,dy$$

### Gradient Vector & Directional Derivative

The **gradient** of a scalar field $f(x, y, z)$ points in the direction of **steepest increase**:

$$\nabla f = \frac{\partial f}{\partial x}\hat{i} + \frac{\partial f}{\partial y}\hat{j} + \frac{\partial f}{\partial z}\hat{k}$$

The rate of change of $f$ in the direction of a vector $\vec{v}$ is the **directional derivative**—always normalize $\vec{v}$ into a **unit vector** $\hat{u} = \frac{\vec{v}}{|\vec{v}|}$ first:

$$D_{\hat{u}} f = \nabla f \cdot \hat{u}$$

The maximum possible rate of change at any point is simply $|\nabla f|$.

### Matrices, Determinants & Cramer's Rule

For a $2 \times 2$ matrix $A = \begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{pmatrix}$:

$$\det(A) = ad - bc, \qquad A^{-1} = \frac{1}{ad - bc}\begin{pmatrix} d & -b \\ -c & a \end{pmatrix} \quad (\det A \neq 0)$$

A square matrix is **singular** (no inverse) if and only if $\det(A) = 0$. For a $3 \times 3$ matrix, expand along the row or column with the most zeros using the checkerboard sign pattern $(+, -, +)$.

<Callout variant="tip" title="Unit vector check for directional derivatives">
If an exam asks for the directional derivative in the direction of $\vec{v} = 3\hat{i} + 4\hat{j}$, DO NOT dot $\nabla f$ with $(3, 4)$ directly! Divide by $|\vec{v}| = \sqrt{3^2 + 4^2} = 5$ first so $\hat{u} = \frac{3}{5}\hat{i} + \frac{4}{5}\hat{j}$.
</Callout>

## 4. Common Exam Pitfalls

<Callout variant="warning" title="Quick checks before submitting">
- **Directional derivative ≠ vector:** $D_{\hat{u}} f = \nabla f \cdot \hat{u}$ is always a **scalar**, whereas $\nabla f$ is a **vector**.
- **Forgetting the minus sign in cofactor expansion:** For $3 \times 3$ determinants, the middle term in row 1 is **subtracted**: $a_{11}C_{11} - a_{12}M_{12} + a_{13}C_{13}$.
</Callout>
